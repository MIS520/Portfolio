Overview

The Brief

The brief centered on Mobile Transfers: how customers find, add, and manage the contacts they send money to, and where that experience breaks down. The institution brought in an experiential learning team to research this space and design improvements, working across a distributed stakeholder group spanning research, design, engineering, and compliance.

Context

Why this mattered

Contact Hub was the working name for a research and design effort inside the institution's Mobile Transfers product: how customers manage the people and accounts they send money to, and where that experience falls short. As an experiential learning engagement, the team was brought in for a fixed period, January to April 2026, to research this space and hand off recommendations the institution's own teams could act on once the engagement ended.

My role was to lead that research and the design work that followed: scoping what we could realistically investigate, building the frameworks the team used to run and analyze interviews, and keeping documentation in a state where it would still make sense to people who hadn't been in the room for the original conversations.

Because of the institution's disclosure and legal requirements, its name, the specific product, and any details that could identify it are withheld from this case study. What's described here is the research and design process itself: how the work was scoped, run, and handed off to the team that would carry it forward.

Process

How I approached it

01

Scoping the research

Worked with the distributed stakeholder group across research, design, engineering, and compliance to define what the research could and couldn't cover. The institution's disclosure rules shaped that scope as much as the product question did.

02

Building interview & analysis frameworks

Built the frameworks the team used to run interviews and synthesize findings consistently, so results from different stakeholders and sessions could be compared against each other rather than treated as one-off conversations.

03

Facilitating workshops

Ran design, feedback, and stakeholder workshops to surface insights from the research and align the group on which opportunity areas mattered most, tying each one back to a measurable business outcome.

04

Maintaining living documentation

Kept documentation updated throughout the engagement so findings stayed accessible to people outside the immediate team. The experiential format meant the team wouldn't be there to explain it later, so the documentation had to.

Key insight

"Research only does its job if someone can still use it after the team that ran it is gone."

That's the constraint an experiential learning engagement puts on the work. There's a fixed end date, and after it, the people who built the context for every finding are no longer in the room. Documentation wasn't an afterthought; it was part of the deliverable, written for stakeholders who would inherit the findings without having sat in on the interviews.

That shaped how the interview and analysis frameworks were built too: not just to capture what people said, but to capture it in a form the next reader could pick up cold.
