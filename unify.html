Overview

The Problem

62% of newcomers to Canada report struggling to find the resources they need: government sites, Reddit threads, and WhatsApp chats all give different answers. On top of that, 70% struggle to adapt because of cultural and language barriers, often without a community to lean on.

Unify set out to centralize trusted settlement information, AI guidance, and a newcomer community in one app.

Context

Why this mattered

Unify was founded by Cedric and Savar as an Enactus venture, after hearing the same story over and over from people new to Canada: confusion, unclear answers, and isolation. The app brings together a personalized settlement checklist, an AI companion grounded in government and settlement sources, short "Learn" lessons, and a community layer: Q&A forums, interest groups, and small-group matching ("Unify Circles").

My role centered on strategy and partnerships: building the network of organizations that would give Unify reach and credibility, and running the research that shaped what the product itself needed to do.

Partners include RBC, EY, Simon Fraser University, Capilano University, Fraser International College, YMCA BC, United Way BC, Vancouver Public Library, Surrey Libraries, Burnaby Public Library, Burnaby Neighbourhood House, Trout Lake Community Centre, Newcomer Jobs Canada, Promise Vancouver, and Big Brothers Big Sisters.

Process

How I approached it

01

Problem validation

Ran 32 user interviews with newcomers to confirm where settlement actually breaks down — scattered information, no roadmap, and isolation came up consistently.

02

Competitor & feature analysis

Analyzed how other newcomer-support platforms structured their "Learn" content. Used the findings to shape the structure and delivery of Unify's 21 modules and 20 task workflows.

03

Partner outreach

Contacted 136 organizations across settlement services, post-secondary institutions, financial institutions, and community orgs. Converted 15 into active partnerships, including RBC, EY, SFU, and YMCA BC.

04

Beta testing

Ran 16 live beta tests with real newcomers to validate the modules, workflows, and partner-sourced content before wider rollout.

Key insight

"For newcomers, information wasn't the gap. Trust was."

Unify's whole premise is that newcomers can't tell which advice is current or relevant to their situation. An app saying "trust us" doesn't solve that — but an app backed by RBC, SFU, and the Vancouver Public Library does.

That reframed partnerships from a distribution channel into the credibility layer the product needed to work at all.

Unify is live on the App Store and at unifysocial.ca, with a community of 250+ newcomers. The 15-partner network spans financial institutions, post-secondary institutions, libraries, and community organizations across Metro Vancouver, giving Unify both distribution and the credibility a new app can't build on its own.

The venture was recognized with three Enactus awards: 1st place at the Desjardin Community Empowerment Challenge (Enactus Canada), 2nd place at the Innovation & Impact Challenge (Enactus Western Canada), and Best Project from the RBC Foundation (Enactus Canada).

Separately, using Figma's MCP integration with Claude, I researched and prototyped a redesign of Unify's AI assistant — work that's still under development and will ship later this year, so I can't share details yet.
